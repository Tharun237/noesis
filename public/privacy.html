Noesis

Privacy

Last changed 13 October 2026

Noesis runs on your own device. Your documents, the audio made from them and your settings are kept in your browser, on that device. Noesis has no advertising, and it does not sell or share information about you. It counts visits to this site, without cookies and without knowing who you are.

If you do not sign in

Nothing about you or your library is stored by Noesis. Five things do go over the network:

  • A web link you add. Browsers do not let one site read another, so Noesis's server fetches the page for you and hands it to your device. The page is not kept on the server.
  • A voice, the first time you use it. Its files are downloaded once from Hugging Face (huggingface.co), which sees that request the way any website sees a visitor. After that the voice works without a connection.
  • A translator, the first time you listen in another language. Its files are downloaded once from Noesis's own store of them on Cloudflare. The translating is then done by your device: the document is not sent anywhere. After that the translator works without a connection.
  • The app itself. Your browser fetches Noesis and its updates from Cloudflare, which hosts it and, like any host, sees the address the request came from.
  • A count of your visit. So that we know whether anyone is using Noesis, each page view is counted by Cloudflare Web Analytics: which page of this site was opened, the site that linked to it, the country, the kind of device and browser, and how fast the page loaded. It sets no cookie, keeps nothing in your browser and does not follow you from one visit to the next, so it cannot tell who you are. It never includes your documents, their names, or anything you listen to. This page itself is not counted.

If you sign in with Google

Signing in is optional. It exists for one purpose: to keep the same library on your phone and your computer.

Your library is then kept in your own Google Drive, in a hidden folder that Google gives to each app. You do not see it among your files, no other app can open it, and Noesis can open nothing else in your Drive. It uses your own Drive storage. What is kept there:

  • the text of your documents and their details (title, author, kind, length);
  • the PDF files you add, so that every one of your devices can show their pages, not only the one you added them on. Deleting a document deletes its file there too;
  • what your own AI key described the figures, charts, diagrams and tables in them as, so that each is described once for all your devices;
  • your place in each document;
  • your bookmarks and notes;
  • how long you listened to each document on each day, at which hours of the day, and on which kind of device (for example "Android phone"), so that your listening is the same on every device;
  • your collections;
  • a few of your settings, so that they are the same on every device: how fast you listen, your usual voice, how your library is sorted, and whether the next document plays by itself and a finished one is archived.

Noesis keeps no copy of any of it. It has no database of accounts or libraries. Audio is never uploaded: each device makes its own audio from the text.

From Google, Noesis receives your name, email address, profile picture and Google account number, used only to show which account is signed in, and a renewal token, which lets the app reach that one folder without asking you to sign in every hour. These are not stored on Noesis's server either: they are kept in a cookie in your own browser, locked with a key only the server has. To renew its hourly pass for your Drive, your browser sends that cookie to Noesis's server, which asks Google and hands the pass back; nothing is written down.

You can sign out at any time in Settings. To remove what is kept, and Noesis's access with it, open Google Drive's settings on the web, choose Manage apps, and under Noesis choose "Delete hidden app data" and then "Disconnect from Drive". Nothing then remains anywhere but on your own devices.

If you turn on AI features

They are off until you add a key of your own, and the key is kept on your device.

  • Google Gemini: the text you ask about goes straight from your device to Google, under your own account with them.
  • NVIDIA: NVIDIA does not accept requests from a browser, so your key and the text pass through Noesis's server on their way. Neither is stored there.
  • Another service you name: the text goes to the address you gave.

What those services do with what they receive is set by your agreement with them, not by Noesis.

If you choose to pay

Noesis is free to use, and paying is voluntary: the Support Noesis page says what the payment is. If you pay, it happens on a page run by Razorpay, a payment company, not inside Noesis.

  • What that page asks for: the amount, your email address and your phone number, and then your card, bank or UPI details.
  • Who receives it: Razorpay, and Aetheral Catch Seafoods, the maker's company, which sees the amount, the email address and the phone number in its Razorpay account. It does not see your card or bank details.
  • What Noesis receives: nothing. The app is not told that you paid, and nothing about your library goes to the payment page.

Your email address and phone number are used for the payment and its receipt, and to answer you if you write about it. They are not sold or shared, and not used to send you anything else. What Razorpay does with what it receives is set by Razorpay's own terms.

Cookies

One, and only if you sign in: the locked cookie described above, which keeps you signed in. There are no tracking cookies.
