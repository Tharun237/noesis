Noesis

PDF to audio, free and on your own device

Add a PDF to Noesis and it becomes audio: a natural voice, made on your own device, reads it to you. You can listen in the app or save the audio as a file. It is free to use, and the PDF is never uploaded.

Open Noesis

How to turn a PDF into audio

  1. Open Noesis in your browser. There is nothing to install and no account to make.
  2. Add the PDF. Its text is read on your device.
  3. Press Play. The first time, the voice downloads once (about 60 MB). After that it works with no connection.
  4. To keep the audio, open the document's menu and choose Save audio once the whole document has been made.

What the audio is like

  • It sounds like prose. Page numbers, running headers and reference lists are left out.
  • A table is described, not read out cell by cell.
  • You choose the voice and the speed, and can change either at any time.
  • The saved file is Opus audio (or WAV in a browser that cannot make Opus). It is not MP3; most players and phones open Opus.

What to know first

  • Your device makes the audio, so a long book takes a while to finish on an older phone. You can start listening after a few seconds; the rest is made ahead of you.
  • A scanned PDF is read from the text stored inside the scan. If that text is poor, the audio will be too.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. The voice runs on your own device, so there is no per-minute cost to pass on. There is no account, no trial and no limit on length.

Is my PDF uploaded anywhere?

No. The PDF is opened and read by your own browser. Only if you sign in, to keep the same library on two devices, is the text kept in your own Google Drive.

Can I get an MP3?

Not an MP3. Save audio gives an Opus file (WAV where a browser cannot make Opus), which phones and most players open.
