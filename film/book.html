The Lighthouse Year

A story written for Noesis

Spring

Mira came to the lighthouse in the first warm week of spring. The sea was bright that morning, and the gulls were loud with joy. She carried one bag, a kettle and a box of books. The old keeper met her at the door with a smile. He was a kind man, and he laughed easily. He showed her the lamp as if it were a gift. It was beautiful, he said, and she would love it by summer. She believed him at once.

The village below was small and friendly. Children ran along the harbour wall and waved to her. A baker left warm bread on the step each Friday. Mira felt lucky, and she said so in her letters home. In the evenings she read aloud to the lamp room. Her own voice kept her good company. The words seemed to shine a little when they were spoken. It was a good, calm way to end a day.

By May the cliffs were covered in flowers. She learned the names of the boats and the friends who sailed them. Each night she lit the lamp, and each night the boats came safe to harbour. It was peaceful work, and she was proud of it. She had never been so happy. The keeper said the light suited her. She thought that it was the best spring of her life. She was right about that.

Summer

Summer made the island gentle. The water was calm for weeks, and the nights were short. Visitors climbed the stairs to admire the view. Mira enjoyed their delight and answered every question. A girl from the village came to help with the brass. Her name was Tess, and her laughter filled the tower. They became friends before the month was out. It was an easy, cheerful friendship.

Tess could not read well, and she was ashamed of it. So Mira read to her while they worked. They read stories of brave sailors and of wonderful, sunny countries. Tess listened with her whole face. She said that hearing a book was better than seeing one. Mira smiled, because she had always thought the same. A story heard is a story shared. That was the happiness of it.

On the longest day the village held a celebration on the beach. There was music, and a great fire, and sweet cakes. The keeper danced, to everyone's surprise and pleasure. Mira watched the lamp turn above the party. She felt safe, and grateful, and free. Nothing in that bright summer warned her of the autumn. The sea kept its secret well.

Autumn

The first storm came in October, in the dark. The wind cried around the tower all night. In the morning the old keeper was sick and could not stand. Mira was afraid, but she did not say so. The doctor came across the rough water and shook his head. It was a bad illness, and the keeper was weak. He died before the week was over. It was a terrible loss.

The funeral was held in the rain. Mira stood alone at the grave after the others had gone. Grief sat in her like cold water. She climbed back to the lighthouse with tears on her face. The lamp room felt empty and wrong without him. She was lonely now in a way she had never been. For many nights she could not read at all. The sadness was too heavy.

Then the worst storm of the year fell on the coast. A fishing boat was lost on the rocks in the darkness. Two men were hurt, and one was never found. People in the village were angry with fear and sorrow. Some said the light had failed for a moment. Mira knew that it had not, and still she felt the guilt. She lay awake with dread, and with the sound of the sea. It was a miserable, frightened time.

Winter

Winter closed around the island like a fist. The nights were long, and the cold was cruel. Ice broke one of the great windows. Mira worked with bleeding hands to mend it before dark. She was hungry, and tired, and alone. The supply boat could not cross for nineteen days. She began to worry that she would fail. Despair is a cold companion.

One evening the lamp went out. The darkness was complete, and the danger was real. Somewhere out there a ship was looking for the harbour. Mira climbed the stairs in a panic, with her heart loud in her ears. Her hands shook so badly that she broke two matches. She thought of the lost boat, and of the keeper's grave. For a moment she was sure that everything was ruined. It was the worst hour of the year.

Then she heard her own voice, reading in the dark. She was saying the lines the old man had loved. The words steadied her hands. The third match caught, and the lamp woke. Far out, a ship's horn answered, low and thankful. Mira sat down on the cold floor and cried. But these were not the tears of autumn.

Spring again

The thaw came early. Tess was the first to climb the hill, and she brought warm bread. She hugged Mira so hard that they both laughed. The village had heard about the ship, and it was proud of its keeper. The baker sent a cake, and the children sent drawings. Mira pinned every one of them to the wall. The tower felt like a home again. There was laughter on the stairs.

In April she started a reading hour in the lamp room. At first only Tess came. Then the fishermen came, shy and smiling, with their caps in their hands. Mira read to them of calm seas and of kind strangers. They listened in a happy silence. Some of them had never heard a whole book before. They said it was a wonderful thing, to be read to. Mira thought so too, and was glad.

On a bright morning in May she walked to the keeper's grave with flowers. She told him that the light was well, and so was she. The sea below was gentle and full of sun. She was not afraid of the autumn any more. Every evening the lamp was lit, and every evening a voice went up the stairs. The boats came home safe. And Mira, who had come with one bag and a kettle, was glad that she had stayed.
